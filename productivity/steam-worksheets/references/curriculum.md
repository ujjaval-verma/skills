# Curriculum reference — Ontario Kindergarten (WRDSB-aligned)

This maps the worksheet topics to the four frames of Ontario's Kindergarten
program and describes what each level expects. Use it to choose a level and to
judge whether a generated sheet is pitched right. It is guidance, not a rigid
standard — match the child in front of you.

## The four frames (context)

Ontario Kindergarten is play-based and organised around four frames rather than
subjects: Belonging & Contributing, Self-Regulation & Well-Being, Demonstrating
Literacy & Mathematics Behaviours, and Problem Solving & Innovating. Worksheets
mainly serve the last two, with the creative/arts activities supporting fine
motor and self-regulation (focus, persistence).

A realistic note on attention: many 5-year-olds focus for 10–15 minutes, which
is why sheets target ~15 minutes. They are built as 3–4 short, varied activities
so a child can work through them and still complete the page in one calm
sitting.

## Literacy

What the program targets: letter recognition (upper/lower), letter–sound
correspondence, name and letter formation, rhyming, early high-frequency words,
and fine-motor control for printing.

- **Level 1** — recognise and trace a single letter; say its sound; connect it
  to a key word (R → rocket).
- **Level 2** — hear and write the middle sound of a CVC word (c_t → "a"); blend
  three sounds to read the word.
- **Level 3** — segment and spell a whole CVC word from a picture; begin simple
  high-frequency words.

## Mathematics

What the program targets: counting to 20, one-to-one correspondence, numeral
formation, comparing quantities, composing/decomposing small numbers, 2-D
shapes, and patterning.

- **Level 1** — count a set to ~5, trace and write the numeral, match quantity
  to symbol.
- **Level 2** — add two small groups within 10 with objects to count.
- **Level 3** — add within 20 more abstractly, without needing to count pictures.

## Patterns (mathematics — algebra strand)

What the program targets: identifying, extending, and creating repeating
patterns, then growing patterns.

- **Level 1** — extend an AB repeating pattern by one element.
- **Level 2** — extend an AAB repeating pattern by two elements (must identify
  the repeating unit first).
- **Level 3** — extend a growing pattern (1, 2, 3, …), an early step toward
  multiplicative thinking.

## Problem solving & innovating

What the program targets: persistence, planning, spatial reasoning, and
following a path — skills the maze/trail activities build.

- **Level 1** — trace a single wide trail from start to finish (fine motor,
  directionality).
- **Level 2** — solve a small maze with dead ends (planning, backtracking).
- **Level 3** — solve a larger maze (sustained focus, spatial memory).

## Arts (creative finisher)

Colour-by-number scenes reinforce numeral recognition and a colour key while
giving a calm, satisfying close to the page. They also build pencil control and
colour vocabulary. Themed scenes (rocket, rangoli, fish) keep it engaging.

## Science / observation (sorting)

"Which one is different?" builds the observation, comparison, and classification
behaviours the program emphasises — noticing attributes and explaining why one
item doesn't belong.

## Choosing a level quickly

- Knows most letters, counts to ~5, just starting to write → **Level 1**.
- Counts and writes numbers confidently, knows letter sounds, blending CVC
  words → **Level 2**.
- Reading simple words, adding small numbers in their head → **Level 3**.

When unsure, start one level lower than you think; success builds momentum, and
the next sheet can step up.
